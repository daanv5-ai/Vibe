Before the first launch

  • Open d-flight.it that morning. Stay under 120 m, in sight, and off people.
  • Cine mode. 4K at 25 fps. Normal color, unless you will grade D-Log M later.
  • Lock white balance. Lock exposure a little under, so the lake does not blow out.
  • Wind limit is about 38 km/h. If the drone fights the hover, land. Morning is the calmer window.
  • Battery in an inside pocket until launch. Land at 30 percent. One battery for the house and meadow, one for the ridge, one for Trarego.
  • Home point in an open patch. Return-to-home above the canopy and under 120 m.
  • The Flip looks forward only. Automated circles turn sensing off. Fly the dusk orbit by hand if trees are close.
  • The Swiss border is up the valley past Sant'Eurosia. Stay on the Trarego side. Power lines follow the SP 92.
  • Shoot each move for 10–12 seconds. Shoot the wide view for 15. Hold still for two seconds at each end.

The house

The house sits in the valley of the song, from 1:18, and again as the melody lands.

Out of the balcony

1:18–1:30 filename house-balcony

Fly out of the open door and let the trees, plus a piece of the view, come with you. If the doorway is tight or under a roof, start a few meters outside with the open door behind the drone, then fly away. Very slow, Cine mode, someone watching the rails.

Past the front

1:30–1:42 filename house-front

A sideways pass along the facade, close enough that the house stays large. End before it becomes a speck. The Flip is about a 24 mm camera, so closeness matters.

Dusk orbit

2:40–3:09 blue hour filename house-dusk

A slow circle in the open air in front of the lit house. Blue hour: warm windows, some color left in the sky, and you can still see the aircraft. Fly it by hand. A green flashing light stays on if it is actually night. Check d-flight again that evening.

If the last phrase still feels bright when you watch the edit, hold the wide view for the last seconds and let the house be only the final cut.

Cut it in CapCut

Use the free CapCut app on the phone or the computer. Skip Pro, skip templates, and skip one-tap AI. Those tools pick their own music.

On an iPhone or Mac, iMovie does the same job and is already installed. On Windows, Clipchamp exports 1080p free. Leave DaVinci Resolve for another film.

  1. Copy the clips off the drone’s card into a folder before you edit. Name them with the filenames on the cards.
  2. New project, 16:9. Drop Luminary on the audio track, starting at zero. You need the audio file, not only the Spotify link. Use a copy you are allowed to use. Turn the clips’ own microphones down.
  3. Drop the clips above the song in the order of the table. Rough lengths are the gaps between the times.
  4. A beat button can mark the pulse. Move those marks to where the violin actually opens or sits down. The pulse and the phrase are not the same.
  5. Play from the start. Drag the edge of each clip so the cut lands when the violin changes. If the phrase turns a second early, follow the phrase.
  6. Plain cuts. A short crossfade only if a join blinks. No whoosh sounds and no zoom effects.
  7. Title on the last two seconds, over the final notes.
  8. Export 1080p on a phone, or 4K if the computer plays the timeline smoothly. Watch it once with the sound up before you erase the card.

A private edit is fine. YouTube and Instagram often mute or claim Luminary.
